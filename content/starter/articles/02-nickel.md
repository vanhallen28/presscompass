Nickel goes into stainless steel and into the batteries of many electric cars. Indonesia has more of it than any other country, and for most of the past decade it has used that position to change where the metal is processed.

## The policy

Since January 2020, Indonesia has banned the export of raw nickel ore. Mining companies must sell their ore to smelters and refineries inside the country, which turn it into products such as nickel pig iron, ferronickel and battery-grade chemicals. An earlier ban, introduced in 2014, was eased in 2017 to give processing plants time to be built. The government calls the approach *hilirisasi*, or downstreaming: earning more by exporting processed goods rather than raw materials.

## The result

Indonesia's output has grown to dominate world supply. The US Geological Survey estimates that Indonesian mines produced about 2.2 million tonnes of nickel in 2024, roughly three-fifths of the world total of 3.7 million tonnes. The country also holds the largest reserves, an estimated 55 million tonnes.

Most of the processing happens in large industrial parks, notably the Indonesia Morowali Industrial Park in Central Sulawesi and the Indonesia Weda Bay Industrial Park in North Maluku. Much of the capital, technology and ownership behind the smelters comes from Chinese companies.

## The dispute

The European Union challenged the ban at the World Trade Organization in November 2019. In a report circulated on 30 November 2022, a WTO panel found that the export ban and the requirement to process ore at home were inconsistent with the General Agreement on Tariffs and Trade and were not justified by its exceptions. Indonesia appealed on 8 December 2022. Because the WTO's Appellate Body has not been functioning, the appeal has not been heard and the dispute remains unresolved.

## Managing a glut

Rapid growth brought more nickel than buyers needed, and prices fell. The government now uses the annual mining work plans and quotas it approves for each company to manage how much ore is dug up, a tool it has used to slow output when prices weaken.

## The costs

Researchers and environmental groups have raised concerns about the coal-fired power plants built to run many smelters, about forest clearing around mines, and about the disposal of processing waste. Supporters of the policy point to new jobs, investment and export earnings in regions that had little industry before. Both sides of that ledger will shape whether downstreaming is copied for other minerals, such as bauxite and copper.
