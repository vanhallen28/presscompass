In 2019, President Joko Widodo announced that Indonesia would move its capital out of Jakarta, a crowded, flood-prone city that is sinking in places, to a new city on the island of Borneo. The site, in East Kalimantan, is about 1,200 km from Jakarta. The city was named Nusantara, an old word for the Indonesian archipelago, and is often referred to by its Indonesian initials, IKN (*Ibu Kota Nusantara*).

## What has been built

The first phase concentrated on a core government area, including the presidential palace and ministry buildings, along with roads, water supply and a toll road linking the site to Balikpapan. In September 2025, AFP reported that about 800 of the 6,600 hectares planned for the core zone had been developed or prepared, that the executive area was nearly complete, and that just over 1,000 staff of the Nusantara Capital Authority were living in the city.

## Slower, smaller, later

Under President Prabowo Subianto, who took office in October 2024, central government spending on the city has fallen sharply, from Rp43.4 trillion in 2024 to Rp6.3 trillion planned for 2026, according to AFP. The government has instead approved a multi-year budget: in 2025 the head of the authority, Basuki Hadimuljono, said about Rp48.8 trillion had been approved for work through 2028, including offices for the legislature and the courts, and housing.

A presidential regulation issued in 2025, Perpres 79/2025, set the new target: Nusantara is to function as the country's *political* capital in 2028, when the three branches of government should be able to work there. That is a narrower goal than the original plan for a full move of the civil service. Under the Nusantara law, the formal transfer of capital status from Jakarta takes effect only when the president signs a decree on the move.

## Private money

The government had hoped private investors would pay for most of the city. By August 2026, the authority said 67 private investors had committed Rp74.54 trillion, about US$4.56 billion, with further public-private partnership projects worth Rp134.22 trillion proposed. Private projects completed or under way include a university campus and a hospital; a new round of partnership projects covers housing and roads.

## The debate

Supporters argue that the move spreads development beyond Java, which holds most of the country's people and economic activity, and that the investment already made should not be wasted. Critics, including some urban planners and economists, warn that a half-finished city could become a "white elephant" and that the money would do more good elsewhere. Environmental groups have raised concerns about forests, wildlife and the rights of Indigenous communities around the site.

The 2028 deadline will be the next real test of whether the plan holds.
