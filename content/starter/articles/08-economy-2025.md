Statistics Indonesia (BPS) reported on 5 February 2026 that gross domestic product grew 5.11 per cent in 2025, up from 5.03 per cent in 2024 and the fastest rate since 2022. At current prices, the economy was worth Rp23,821.1 trillion. Growth was close to, but slightly below, the government's target for the year.

## Households did most of the work

Household consumption is the largest part of Indonesia's economy, at 53.88 per cent of GDP in 2025. It grew 4.98 per cent and contributed 2.62 percentage points of the 5.11 per cent total, roughly half the growth. Spending at restaurants and hotels rose faster than average, by 6.38 per cent.

Growth in household spending of just under 5 per cent is in line with recent years. It suggests demand held up, but not that it accelerated sharply.

## Investment in machinery picked up

Gross fixed capital formation, the official name for investment in buildings, machinery and equipment, accounted for 28.77 per cent of GDP. It grew 5.09 per cent and added 1.58 percentage points to growth. Within it, investment in machinery and equipment jumped 17.99 per cent, a sign that companies were adding productive capacity rather than only building.

## Factories did better

On the production side, manufacturing grew 5.30 per cent and contributed 1.07 percentage points to growth, more than any other sector. Manufacturing has often grown more slowly than the economy as a whole in the past decade, so a year in which it kept pace is welcome news for job creation.

## What to watch

Economists will look at whether the investment pick-up continues, whether household incomes keep pace with prices, and how government spending programmes shape demand. BPS publishes GDP figures every quarter, about five weeks after the quarter ends, and updates earlier figures as more data comes in.
