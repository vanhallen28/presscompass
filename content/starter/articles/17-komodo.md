The Komodo dragon (*Varanus komodoensis*) is the largest lizard on Earth. Adults can grow to about three metres long. It lives nowhere else but Indonesia, and only in the province of East Nusa Tenggara: on the islands of Komodo National Park and along parts of the coast of Flores.

## How many there are

According to the Ministry of Forestry, about 3,200 Komodo dragons live in Komodo National Park, a population officials describe as stable. About 700 more live outside the park, in the north and west of Flores.

The park was established in 1980 to protect the dragons and their habitat, and was listed as a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 1991. Its best-known islands are Komodo, Rinca and Padar, and its waters are rich in coral reefs and marine life.

## The threats

The species is classified as Endangered on the Red List of the International Union for Conservation of Nature. Inside the park, the dragons are protected. Outside it, the picture is different: about half of their range on Flores lies in areas without formal protection, where officials say the main threats are:

- **loss and damage of habitat**, as land is cleared for farms, roads and settlements;
- **fewer prey animals**, because deer and wild pigs are hunted illegally;
- **tourism growth** in some areas, which brings more people and building into dragon habitat.

## The plan

In 2025, the government signed a national Komodo Conservation Strategy and Action Plan for 2025 to 2035, prepared with conservation groups including the Komodo Survival Program. It sets out four programmes to protect the dragons and their prey both inside and outside protected areas.

Conservationists say the hardest work is outside the park, with the villages that have lived beside the dragons for generations. "The conservation of Komodo dragons is urgent for all of us," said Ahmad Munawir, the ministry's director of species and genetic conservation, in February 2026.
