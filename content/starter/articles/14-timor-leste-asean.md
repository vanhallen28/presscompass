On 26 October 2025, at the 47th ASEAN Summit in Kuala Lumpur, the bloc's leaders signed a declaration admitting Timor-Leste as its eleventh member. Malaysia's prime minister, Anwar Ibrahim, signed first as that year's chair, followed by the other leaders and Timor-Leste. "Today, history is made," said Timor-Leste's prime minister, Xanana Gusmão.

## A long wait

Timor-Leste applied to join in 2011. ASEAN had last admitted a member in 1999, when Cambodia joined. Before full membership, the bloc agreed in principle to admit Timor-Leste, gave it observer status and set out a road map of requirements it had to meet, from joining ASEAN agreements to preparing its civil service.

ASEAN now has eleven members: Brunei, Cambodia, Indonesia, Laos, Malaysia, Myanmar, the Philippines, Singapore, Thailand, Timor-Leste and Vietnam.

## Indonesia's neighbour

Timor-Leste shares the island of Timor with Indonesia, whose province of East Nusa Tenggara covers the western half. Its history is closely tied to Indonesia's: the territory was governed by Indonesia from 1976 until a referendum organised by the United Nations in 1999, in which a large majority voted for independence. It became an independent state in 2002.

## What membership brings

As a member, Timor-Leste takes part in ASEAN's summits and ministerial meetings, joins its free trade arrangements over time, and gains a voice in a bloc that takes decisions by consensus. For a country of about 1.4 million people with a small, oil-dependent economy, supporters of membership say access to regional markets, investment and training is the main prize. The challenge is capacity: a small civil service now has to keep up with ASEAN's busy calendar of summits, ministerial meetings and working groups.
