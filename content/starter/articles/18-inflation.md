Statistics Indonesia (BPS) measures inflation from the prices of a basket of goods and services that households buy, collected in cities across the country every month. The headline number most people quote is the year-on-year rate for December, which shows how much prices rose over the whole calendar year.

Inflation was low during the pandemic years of 2020 and 2021, as demand fell. It jumped to 5.51 per cent in 2022, the highest since 2014, after the government raised subsidised fuel prices in September that year and global food and energy prices surged. It then fell back to 2.61 per cent in 2023 and to 1.57 per cent in 2024, the lowest annual rate since 1958.

## What pushed prices up in 2025

In December 2025 alone, prices rose 0.64 per cent from November, and the year-on-year rate reached 2.92 per cent. Food, drink and tobacco was the group that added most, contributing 0.48 percentage points in the month:

- bird's eye chillies added 0.17 points;
- chicken meat 0.09 points;
- shallots 0.07 points;
- fresh fish 0.04 points, and eggs 0.03 points.

Over the year as a whole, The Jakarta Post reported that food and gold jewellery, whose price follows the world gold price, were among the main drivers.

## Why it matters

Low and stable inflation protects the incomes of households, especially poorer ones, who spend a large share of their money on food. Bank Indonesia aims to keep inflation within a target range of 1.5 to 3.5 per cent, and adjusts interest rates partly with that range in mind. The government, for its part, tries to steady food prices through the rice reserve and other supply measures, because a few staples such as chillies, shallots and rice can move the whole index in a single month.
