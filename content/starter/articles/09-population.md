Indonesia is the world's fourth most populous country. According to the Directorate General of Population and Civil Registration (Dukcapil) at the Home Affairs Ministry, it had about 290.1 million registered residents at the end of June 2026.

## Two ways to count

Indonesia has two main sources of population figures. Dukcapil counts the people recorded in the civil registry, which issues the national identity number (NIK) and the family card, and publishes totals twice a year. Statistics Indonesia (BPS) runs the census every ten years and publishes projections in between; its projection for 2025 was 284.4 million. The two figures differ because they measure different things: registrations on one side, and an estimate of the people actually living in each place on the other.

## Java first

Six provinces have more than 10 million residents, and five of them are on Java.

West Java alone has more than 52 million residents, more than most countries in Europe. East Java follows with 42.2 million and Central Java with 38.6 million. Outside Java, the largest provinces are North Sumatra, with 15.8 million, and South Sulawesi, with 9.6 million.

## And the smallest

At the other end, West Papua had about 588,000 registered residents, the fewest of any province. The provinces of Papua and Maluku cover a large part of the country's land but hold a small share of its people.

## Why it matters

Population shapes how much the central government sends to each region, how many seats each province has in parliament, and where schools, hospitals and roads are needed. The number of provinces has also grown: four new provinces were created in Papua in 2022, bringing the total to 38, which changed both the regional map and the size of the Regional Representative Council.
