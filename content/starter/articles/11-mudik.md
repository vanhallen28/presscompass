*Mudik* is the Indonesian word for the journey home that millions of people make before Idul Fitri, known locally as Lebaran, the festival that ends the fasting month of Ramadan. People who have moved to the cities for work return to their parents' villages and towns to pray together, ask forgiveness of their elders and share the holiday meal.

## How many people travel

The Ministry of Transportation surveys the public each year to estimate how many people will travel. For Lebaran 2026, the minister told a cabinet meeting on 13 March that about 143.91 million people were expected to be on the move, equal to 50.6 per cent of the population.

The survey estimate is usually conservative. For 2025, the ministry had projected about 146 million travellers, but the actual number reached about 154 million, according to the minister.

## Getting there

Private cars carry the largest share of travellers, followed by motorcycles and buses. The busiest gateways include Soekarno-Hatta Airport near Jakarta, the Merak–Bakauheni ferry crossing between Java and Sumatra, Pasar Senen railway station and the Pulogebang bus terminal in Jakarta.

Because so many people leave the capital at once, the authorities use several tools to keep traffic moving:

- **One-way and contraflow lanes** on the main toll roads at the busiest times.
- **Flexible working arrangements**, which let civil servants and some private employees work from elsewhere for a few days before the holiday, so departures are spread out.
- **A police traffic operation**, called Operasi Ketupat, with checkpoints, rest areas and emergency services along the main routes.

## Why it matters

Mudik is often described as one of the largest regular human migrations in the world. It moves money from the cities to the regions, as travellers spend on transport, food and gifts, and send cash to relatives. It also tests the country's roads, railways, ports and airports, and every year brings warnings about road safety, especially for families travelling long distances by motorcycle.
