Indonesia stretches across more than 5,000 kilometres of ocean and islands, from Aceh in the west to Papua in the east. To keep clocks roughly in line with the sun, the country uses three time zones, each one hour apart. None of them observes daylight saving time, which would make little sense so close to the equator, where day length barely changes through the year.

## The three zones

- **Western Indonesia Time (WIB), UTC+7:** all the provinces of Sumatra, the Riau Islands, Bangka Belitung, Java (including Jakarta, Banten and Yogyakarta), and West and Central Kalimantan.
- **Central Indonesia Time (WITA), UTC+8:** South, East and North Kalimantan; all the provinces of Sulawesi, including Gorontalo; Bali; and West and East Nusa Tenggara.
- **Eastern Indonesia Time (WIT), UTC+9:** Maluku, North Maluku and the six provinces of Papua.

About four in five Indonesians live in the western zone, so national television schedules and stock-market trading run on Jakarta time, and people further east often adjust.

## How the lines were drawn

The current arrangement was set by a presidential decree, Keppres 41/1987, signed on 26 November 1987, and took effect on 1 January 1988. It moved Bali from the western to the central zone and West and Central Kalimantan from the central to the western zone.

Before that, time in the archipelago was changed many times. Under Dutch rule there were at one stage six zones, half an hour apart. During the Japanese occupation, from 1942 to 1945, much of the country was put on Tokyo time. The present three-zone system dates from 1964, and the 1988 change redrew its boundaries.

## The one-zone idea

In 2012 the government proposed putting the whole country on a single time zone at UTC+8, the same as Singapore, Malaysia, Hong Kong and China. The coordinating minister for the economy at the time, Hatta Rajasa, argued that shared business hours across the archipelago could save trillions of rupiah. For Sumatra, Java and western Kalimantan, clocks would have moved an hour forward; for Maluku and Papua, an hour back. The plan, which had been given target dates in August and October 2012, was put on hold and has not been revived.
