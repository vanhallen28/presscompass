On 6 January 2025, Brazil, which chaired BRICS that year, announced that Indonesia had joined the group as a full member. It was the first Southeast Asian country to do so. BRICS leaders had approved Indonesia's candidacy in 2023, but Jakarta asked to wait until after its 2024 general election and the formation of a new government.

## What BRICS is

BRICS began as an informal grouping of large emerging economies: Brazil, Russia, India and China, joined by South Africa in 2010. It expanded in 2024 to include Egypt, Ethiopia, Iran and the United Arab Emirates. With Indonesia, it has ten members.

The group is not a treaty alliance or a trade bloc. It has no common market and no defence commitments. Its members meet at annual summits, coordinate positions on issues such as reform of international financial institutions, and run the New Development Bank, based in Shanghai.

## Why Indonesia joined

Indonesia's foreign policy has been described since independence as *bebas aktif*, free and active: the country avoids formal alliances and tries to work with all sides. The government presents BRICS membership in those terms, as a way to strengthen cooperation among developing countries, widen its trade and investment ties, and give it another forum in which to argue for changes to global economic rules. It has stressed that joining does not mean taking sides.

## The OECD track

At the same time, Indonesia is trying to join the Organisation for Economic Co-operation and Development, a club of mostly high-income democracies whose members agree to align many laws and policies with OECD standards. The OECD opened accession talks with Indonesia in February 2024 and adopted a roadmap for them in May 2024. Accession usually takes years, as each OECD committee reviews the candidate's policies in its field.

Indonesia argues that the two memberships are compatible: one supports cooperation among emerging economies, the other provides a benchmark for domestic reform.

## The questions

Some analysts and Western diplomats have asked whether membership of a group that includes Russia and Iran, and in which China is the largest economy, could complicate Indonesia's relationships with the United States and Europe. Others reply that India and Brazil, both BRICS founders, keep close ties with Western countries, and that Indonesia already deals with all the major powers through ASEAN and the G20.

## ASEAN at the centre

Whatever happens with BRICS and the OECD, ASEAN remains the base of Indonesian diplomacy. Indonesia was one of five founding members when the Bangkok Declaration was signed on 8 August 1967, and the ASEAN Secretariat is in Jakarta. How the country balances these memberships is likely to be a running theme of its foreign policy for years.
