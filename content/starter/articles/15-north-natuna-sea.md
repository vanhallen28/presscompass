The Natuna Islands lie in the Riau Islands province, between the Malay Peninsula and Borneo. Under the United Nations Convention on the Law of the Sea, Indonesia's exclusive economic zone, where it alone has the right to fish and to explore for oil and gas, stretches far to the north of them.

## The name

On 14 July 2017, Indonesia published a new official map that gave the northern part of that zone a name of its own: the North Natuna Sea. China protested, saying that changing the name made no sense. Indonesia replied that the name covered only waters within its own jurisdiction.

## Where the claims meet

China claims most of the South China Sea within a U-shaped line on its maps, often called the nine-dash line. At its southern end, the line runs into the waters off the Natuna Islands. In 2016, an international arbitral tribunal, in a case brought by the Philippines, found that China's claim based on the line had no basis in international law. China does not recognise the ruling.

Indonesia's long-standing position is that it is not a claimant in the territorial disputes over the islands and reefs of the South China Sea, which involve China, the Philippines, Vietnam, Malaysia, Brunei and Taiwan, and that it has no overlapping maritime claims with China, because it does not recognise the nine-dash line.

## Boats at sea

The question is practical for fishers and patrols. Chinese fishing boats, sometimes escorted by coast guard ships, have been found in Indonesia's zone off Natuna, and Indonesian patrols have confronted them. In January 2020, after one such incident, President Joko Widodo visited the islands to underline Indonesia's sovereign rights there.

## The 2024 statement

In November 2024, during President Prabowo Subianto's visit to Beijing, the two governments issued a joint statement that spoke of "joint development in areas of overlapping claims". Some analysts warned that the wording could be read as Indonesia accepting that China had a claim in its waters. Indonesia's foreign ministry responded that its position had not changed, that the Chinese claims "have no international legal basis", and that the cooperation "does not impact sovereignty, sovereign rights or Indonesia's jurisdiction in the North Natuna Sea".
