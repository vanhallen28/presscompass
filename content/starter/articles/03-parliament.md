Indonesia's legislature is often called a two-chamber parliament, but it has three names. Voters elect members to the House of Representatives (DPR) and to the Regional Representative Council (DPD). Together, those members form the People's Consultative Assembly (MPR).

## The DPR: laws, budget and oversight

The House of Representatives is the main lawmaking body. Its 580 members for 2024–2029 were elected from party lists in the general election held every five years. Under the Constitution, the DPR holds the power to make laws, but every bill must be discussed and agreed by the DPR and the president together before it becomes law.

The Constitution gives the DPR three functions: legislation, the state budget, and oversight of the government. To carry out oversight it can question the government, open inquiries and state its opinion on government policy. It also approves many senior appointments, such as members of independent commissions.

## The DPD: the regions' voice

The Regional Representative Council has four members from each of Indonesia's 38 provinces, 152 in all. Candidates stand as individuals rather than for parties. The Constitutional Court confirmed in August 2024 that the four-per-province rule set by law is constitutional.

The DPD cannot pass laws on its own. It may propose bills to the DPR, and take part in discussing them, on regional autonomy, relations between the centre and the regions, the creation and merging of regions, natural resources, and the financial balance between Jakarta and the regions. It also gives the DPR its views on the state budget and on bills about tax, education and religion.

## The MPR: the Constitution and the presidency

The People's Consultative Assembly is made up of every DPR and DPD member. Before the constitutional amendments of 1999 to 2002, it was the country's highest state body and elected the president. Today the president and vice-president are elected directly, and the MPR's main powers are narrower:

- it can amend and enact the Constitution;
- it inaugurates the president and vice-president;
- it can remove the president or vice-president during their term, but only through the procedure set out in the Constitution, which requires the Constitutional Court to rule on the DPR's case first.

## Why the numbers change

The size of the DPR is set by election law and has grown with the population and the number of provinces. The DPD grows whenever new provinces are created: four provinces were added in Papua in 2022, which brought the total to 38 and the DPD to 152 members from the 2024 election.
