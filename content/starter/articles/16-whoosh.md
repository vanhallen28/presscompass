Whoosh runs for about 142 kilometres between Jakarta and Bandung, at speeds of up to 350 kilometres an hour. President Joko Widodo inaugurated it on 2 October 2023, years behind the original 2019 target, and commercial service began on 17 October 2023. It was the first high-speed railway in Southeast Asia and is part of China's Belt and Road Initiative.

## Who owns it

The line is run by PT Kereta Cepat Indonesia China (KCIC). An Indonesian consortium of state-owned companies, PT Pilar Sinergi BUMN Indonesia, owns 60 per cent; its largest shareholder is the state railway company, PT Kereta Api Indonesia. A Chinese consortium, Beijing Yawan HSR, owns the remaining 40 per cent. Most of the money to build it was borrowed.

## Passengers

Ridership has grown steadily. By October 2025, two years after opening, KCIC said the trains had carried more than 12 million passengers on nearly 40,000 trips, with a record 26,770 riders in a single day in June 2025 and an on-time rate of 99.9 per cent.

## The money problem

The project cost $7.27 billion, well above the original estimate, and ticket revenue has not been enough to cover interest and repayments. The losses fall largely on the Indonesian state companies in the consortium. According to The Jakarta Post, the railway lost Rp4.9 trillion in 2025 and Rp5.1 trillion in the first half of 2026 alone.

In 2025, Danantara, the state investment agency that now holds the government's shares in state companies, began talks on restructuring the debt. In August 2026, Finance Minister Purbaya Yudhi Sadewa said his ministry would take over the Indonesian consortium from Danantara, while adding that the railway's debt would not be paid with state funds. Coordinating Economic Affairs Minister Airlangga Hartarto said the Chinese side had responded well to the plan.

## The debate

Supporters point to the time saved, the ridership and the hope that new towns around the stations will bring property and business income. Critics argue that the line was too expensive for the traffic between two cities already linked by road, rail and air, and that its debts show the risks of large borrowed infrastructure projects. Extending the line east to Surabaya has been discussed, but no decision has been taken while the finances are being settled.
